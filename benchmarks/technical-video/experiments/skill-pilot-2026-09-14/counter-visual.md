# Independent visual A/B diagnostic

1. Method and limits
   - Read only each topic's brief, source notes, scene map, and A/B video. No mappings, prior reviews, author records, or code were consulted.
   - Extracted and inspected a midpoint frame for every scene: queue A 11 / B 12; UT A 13 / B 11. Also inspected six-sample strips at 0.5-second spacing and six truly consecutive 30 fps frames for each video. Images are saved alongside this report.
   - This is a focused visual sample, not a full playback or exhaustive numerical audit. No audio was listened to; narration quality, synchronization, and learning effectiveness are unassessed. Choices below are diagnostic preferences, not measured learner outcomes.

2. Queue: overall A, narrowly
   - Visual causal clarity: B. At 114–116.5s, the full queue closes an explicit admission gate; service tokens leave, completed count rises 6→8, and the gate opens. This makes the actuator clearer than A's capacity-return arrow. At 239.5s B also gives request 11 a concrete offer/admit/complete timeline and 7−4=3 ticks, whereas A 225s explains latency with a largely generic timeline.
   - State/label consistency: A. At 97.5–100s A separately labels two queued, two admitting/in transit, and two waiting upstream. These labels clarify the intermediate categories. B's 115–116.167s tokens pass over the producer caption and the consumer's “2 items / tick”; some offered tokens initially fade in while cumulative offers already reads 15. B's journey at 28.5s shows a “thumbnail complete ✓” badge while the highlighted request is still waiting in the queue, making an apparent state label premature. It can be read as an output destination, but the check mark invites the wrong interpretation.
   - Readability: A. Its larger spatial separation and separate completion counter remain clean at 29.5s, 99.5s, and 149s. B's smaller labels and token/text overlap at 116s are a concrete disadvantage; this is not simply a preference for light backgrounds.
   - Top-down explanatory structure: A, narrowly. Both complete an individual journey before arithmetic (A 37–39.5s, B 35–37.5s). A then continues the same trace when arrivals change (75s) and explicitly replays its tick when opening the computation (125s). B's model preview at 50.5s precedes a fresh restarted trace at 70.5s, adding a reset to track. Both identify illustrations.
   - Strongest evidence against A: A 99.000–99.167s already says two waiting and two in transit while all four tokens remain spatially within the producer. The labels repair this, but spatial state and semantic state are not perfectly synchronized. B is stronger on explicit admission control, item identity in conservation (165s), and numeric end-to-end latency. If causal mechanism were weighted above presentation/state legibility, B could reasonably win.

3. Universal Transformers: overall B, moderately
   - Visual causal clarity: tie. B makes generation's repeat/append loop concrete early (44–46.5s), shows parallel token positions across depth (89.5s), and uses a clean recurrence arrow in one revision (116.5–131.5s). A is materially stronger inside a refinement: its complete step at 99.88s and residual explanation at 144.98s expose both operands and normalization, while B uses “residual addition + normalization” badges without drawing those paths.
   - State/label consistency: tie. Both consistently distinguish encoder-only views, illustrative states, separate encoder/decoder parameters, and fixed-depth versus optional ACT. B's green encoder/orange decoder and prefix tokens provide strong continuity; A's bank/river example, token indices, and explicit inner/outer loop labels also hold together. No decisive contradictory state claim was found in this sample.
   - Readability: B. The three-token example and straighter flow leave more space at 89.5s, 116.5s, 176s, and 203s. A's six-token example and folded residual routes carry more local detail but demand more visual tracing at 99.88s and 192.17s. Both have small secondary text; B's title briefly appears clipped during its entry at 132.033–132.067s, resolving by 132.5s. This is a short transition issue, not persistent unreadability.
   - Top-down explanatory structure: B. B completes a visible output through an end token by 44s, explains full architecture at 63s, shows refinement, returns to decoder at 147s, then closes training at 176s before opening attention at 203s. A also completes output by 36s, but takes several deeper refinement/attention/residual/transition scenes before its decoder and training (192.17s and 217.45s). B better preserves the requested whole-flow-before-pieces ordering.
   - Strongest evidence against B: A's “bank” ambiguity gives attention an immediate semantic reason at 10.55s, and its 123.38s weighted mixture is anchored to that same ambiguity. B's “cat sleeps” is easier to fit but less explanatory of why contextual refinement matters. A also exposes residual mechanics substantially better; B's labels name operations that its diagram does not unpack. A would be the stronger choice if detailed mechanism reconstruction were prioritized over initial whole-system orientation.
