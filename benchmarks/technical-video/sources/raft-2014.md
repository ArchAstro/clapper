# In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm,2014, Figure2 and §5.2

Source: https://raft.github.io/raft.pdf

Pack type: curator-written reference notes, not a verbatim copy of the primary publication. Freeze/approve these notes before confirmatory evaluation.

Raft servers are followers, candidates or leaders. Terms are monotonically increasing. A follower starts an election after its randomized election timeout, increments its term, votes for itself and sends RequestVote messages.
A candidate needs a majority of all servers in the configuration, not a majority of currently reachable servers. Each server gives at most one vote per term; the log-up-to-date restriction also applies.
An equal-term legitimate leader causes a candidate to return to follower. A higher term updates currentTerm and causes a follower transition. A partition can cause multiple apparent leaders in different terms, not two legitimately elected leaders in the same term.
Leader election is distinct from log replication and commitment. Do not infer that an entry is committed merely because a leader was elected. Use five servers with a3/2 partition as the illustrative case; a two-server minority cannot elect a new leader by itself.
